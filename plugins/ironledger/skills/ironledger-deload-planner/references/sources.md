# Sources for the deload planner skill

Quotes are copied from the sources as read on 18 October 2026.

## Research

**Bell L, Strafford BW, Coleman M, Androulakis Korakakis P, Nolan D. Integrating Deloading into Strength and Physique Sports Training Programmes: An International Delphi Consensus Approach. Sports Medicine - Open 2023;9:87.** https://doi.org/10.1186/s40798-023-00633-0

- "Expert strength and physique coaches were invited to an online Delphi consisting of 3 rounds."

- "Deloading is a period of reduced training stress designed to mitigate physiological and psychological fatigue, promote recovery, and enhance preparedness for subsequent training."
- "there was universal agreement that training volume should be decreased during deloading."
- "Panellists agreed that training intensity could either increase or decrease during deloading."
- "Deloading could be pre-planned and organized around the athlete's lifestyle or integrated using an autoregulatory approach."
- "There was agreement that main exercises could be adapted by using a lower volume or lower training intensity and that assistance/accessory exercises could be adapted or removed altogether."
- "According to the panel, deloading could occur anywhere in the training programme, while the taper would only occur only before a competition."

**Bell L, Nolan D, Immonen V, Helms E, Dallamore J, Wolf M, Androulakis Korakakis P. "You can't shoot another bullet until you've reloaded the gun": Coaches' perceptions, practices and experiences of deloading in strength and physique sports. Frontiers in Sports and Active Living 2022;4:1073223.** https://doi.org/10.3389/fspor.2022.1073223

Interviews with 18 coaches. The guidelines are the authors' interpretation of what the coaches said.

- "There was no single method of deloading; instead, a reduction in training volume (achieved through a reduction in repetitions per set and number of sets per training session) and intensity of effort (increased proximity to failure and/or reduction in relative load) were the most adapted training variables, along with alterations in exercise selection and configuration."
- "The exact periodicity described by participants was broad (ranging from 3 to 12 weeks)."
- Guidelines: "Whilst some practitioners might consider a reduction in training days during the deload, training frequency will typically remain unchanged (relative to normal training frequency)."
- "A reduction in training volume by approximately 30%–50%, achieved through a decrease in either repetitions per set or by a reduction in the number of sets per training session (or in some cases, both)."
- "A reduction in the intensity of effort can be achieved by increasing proximity to muscular failure e.g., by adding 1–3 RIR for each set performed, by removing repetitions per set whilst maintaining absolute load or by reducing the absolute load (e.g., by 10%) whilst keeping the number of repetitions constant."
- "Whilst it is important to approach the duration of the deload on an individual basis, most deloads will last 5 to 7 days."
- "For pre-planned training programmes, deloading can be scheduled every 4 to 8 weeks depending on the training demands of the mesocycle."

**Rogerson D, Nolan D, Androulakis Korakakis P, Immonen V, Wolf M, Bell L. Deloading Practices in Strength and Physique Sports: A Cross-sectional Survey. Sports Medicine - Open 2024;10:26.** https://doi.org/10.1186/s40798-024-00691-y

Survey of 246 competitive strength and physique athletes.

- "The typical duration of a deload was 6.4 ± 1.7 days, integrated into the training programme every 5.6 ± 2.3 weeks. Deloading was undertaken using a proactive, pre-planned strategy (or in combination with an autoregulated approach) and undertaken when performance stalled or during periods of increased muscle soreness or joint aches."
- "Most participants reported that the number of weekly training sessions would not change (63.0%) or would decrease (32.9%) during deloading."
- "Most athletes stated that the number of weekly sets would decrease (78.9%) or remain unchanged (17.9%), and that the number of repetitions performed within each set would also likely decrease (52.8%) or remain the same (30.9%)."
- "Athletes stated that there would be a decrease in training intensity for both multi-joint exercises (83.7%) and single-joint exercises (60.2%)."
- "Most athletes (65.0%) stated that they felt they could progress in their training without deloading."

**Coleman M, Burke R, Augustin F, Piñero A, Maldonado J, Fisher JP, Israetel M, Androulakis Korakakis P, Swinton P, Oberlin D, Schoenfeld BJ. Gaining more from doing less? The effects of a one-week deload period during supervised resistance training on muscular adaptations. PeerJ 2024;12:e16777.** https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.16777

- Thirty-nine resistance-trained young men and women; the deload group "abstained from RT for 1 week at the midpoint of a 9-week, high-volume RT program".
- "TRAD showed greater improvements in both isometric and dynamic lower body strength compared to DELOAD."
- "when trying to optimize increases in maximal strength, periods of complete training cessation likely should be used more sparingly."
- "our results are specific to a deload involving a cessation of RT."

## Programs and coaches

**Jim Wendler, "5/3/1: How to Build Pure Strength", T Nation, 7 July 2009.** https://t-nation.com/t/5-3-1-how-to-build-pure-strength/281694

- "Week 4 – Deload": "40% x 5", "50% x 5", "60% x 5".
- "You're giving your muscles a break, not trying to establish new PRs."
- On the one-rep max: "The calculations are based on 90% of it."

**Starting Strength, Get Started FAQ** (archived copy, 8 January 2025). https://web.archive.org/web/20250108162817/https://startingstrength.com/get-started/faq

- "When you don't make the prescribed sets and reps in a given exercise, try again the next time that exercise comes up. If you make the lift, continue progressing. If you miss again, de-load your weight in that lift by 8-10%, and work your way back up from there, using small jumps."

**Mike Israetel, "Fatigue Explained", Juggernaut Training Systems, 27 September 2013.** https://www.jtsstrength.com/fatigue-explained/

- The deload "is usually about 1 week long".
- "the average trainer should probably deload every 4-6 weeks or so"
- "During a deload week, training volumes must be brought down, probably by half or more of the normal training volumes"
- "Intensity can actually stay up in the usual range during this week ... dropping it by 50% for the last half of the deload week may be a good start."

## Tapering

**Travis SK, Mujika I, Gentles JA, Stone MH, Bazyler CD. Tapering and Peaking Maximal Strength for Powerlifting Performance: A Review. Sports 2020;8(9):125.** https://doi.org/10.3390/sports8090125

- "strength athletes tapering to improve powerlifting performance should (1) reduce training volume by approximately 30–70%, (2) maintain training intensity ≥85% 1RM or reduce training intensity while (3) using either an exponential or step taper model ... over a 1–2 week period followed by (4) a short-term training cessation spanning 2–7 days."
