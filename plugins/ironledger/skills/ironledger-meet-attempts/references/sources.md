# Sources for the meet attempts skill

Quotes are copied from the sources as read on 18 October 2026.

## Federation rules

**International Powerlifting Federation, IPF Technical Rulebook, effective 1 March 2026.** https://www.powerlifting.sport/fileadmin/ipf/data/rules/technical-rules/english/2026_IPF_Technical_Rulebook__effective_01_March_2026__v3.pdf

- "In IPF recognized competitions, the weight of the barbell must always be a multiple of 2.5 kg. Unless attempts are made on records, in Squat, Bench Press and Deadlift, the progression must be at least 2.5 kg between all attempts"
- "In a record attempt the weight of the barbell must be at least 0.5 kg in excess of the current record."
- 6.1(a): "At the weigh in, the lifter or his coach must declare a starting weight for all three lifts."
- 6.1(e): "The bar must be loaded progressively during each round on the principle of a rising bar."
- 6.1(g): "If unsuccessful with an attempt, the lifter does not follow himself, but must wait until the next round before he can attempt that weight again."
- 6.1(i): "A lifter is permitted one change of weight on the first attempt of each lift. The change of weight may be higher or lower than that originally submitted and the order of lifting in the first round will change accordingly. If he is in the first group, this change may take place at any time up to within three minutes before the start of the first round of that lift. The following groups are accorded a similar privilege up to within three attempts from the end of the previous group's last round of that lift."
- 6.1(j): "A lifter must submit his second and third attempts within one minute of completing his preceding attempt. The one minute will begin from the time that the lights are activated. If no weight is submitted within the one-minute time allowance, the lifter will be granted a 2.5 kg increase on his next attempt. Should the lifter have failed his previous attempt and not submitted a weight for a further attempt within the one-minute time allowance, then the bar will be loaded to the failed weight."
- 6.1(k): "Weights submitted for second round attempts on all three lifts cannot be changed. Similarly, third round attempts on the squat and bench press cannot be changed."
- 6.1(l): "In the third round of the deadlift, two changes are permitted. The change of the weight may be higher or lower than the lifters previously submitted third attempt. However, these are only permitted provided that the lifter has not been called to the bar already loaded to his previously submitted weight by the speaker."
- Equipment: "Weight of the bar and collars are to be 25 kg." Collars "Must weigh 2.5 kg each."

**USA Powerlifting, USAPL Rulebook, version 2026.2 (updated 9 February 2026, in effect 1 January 2026).** https://www.usapowerlifting.com/assets/general/PDFs/USAPL-Rulebook-v2026.2.pdf

- 5.4.8: "The weight of the barbell must always be a multiple of 2.5kg (or 5lb in meets being run in pounds)."
- 5.3.1.1: "Lifters are permitted one change of weight on the first attempt of each lift." "If the lifter is in the first flight, this change may take place at any time up to 3 minutes before the start of the first round of that lift."
- 5.3.14: "A lifter must submit their second attempt within 1 minute of completing their first attempt and they must submit their third attempt within 1 minute of completing their second attempt. The 1- minute clock starts when the referee lights are activated."
- 5.3.14.1: "A lifter may never submit an attempt that is lower than the previous attempt in a given discipline/lift. A lifter may (but is not required to) submit the same attempt as the previous attempt in a given discipline/lift when the previous attempt was unsuccessful."
- 5.3.14.2: "If the next attempt is not submitted at all or is not submitted within the 1-minute time allowance, the score table official must enter an increase of 2.5kg (or 5lb in meets being run in pounds) for the next attempt (if the previous attempt was successful) or the weight of the previous attempt will be entered for the next attempt (if the previous attempt was unsuccessful)."
- 5.3.17: "Weights submitted for second and third round attempts cannot be changed once they are submitted". 5.3.17.1: "Weights submitted for third round deadlift attempts (or third round attempts in single-lift bench press or deadlift competition) may be changed twice."
- 2.3.5: "The weight of the bar must be 20kg". 2.5.2: "Collars must weigh 2.5kg each". 2.3.5.1: "The use of a 45lb bar (allowable range: 44.8875 - 45.1125lb) is permitted at local competitions provided it is used with pound plates and pound collars". 2.5.2.2: "The use of 5lb collars (allowable range: 4.978 - 5.022lb) is permitted at local competitions provided they are used with a 45lb bar and pound plates."
- 2.12.3: "For youth lifters, the weight of the barbell may be any multiple of 0.5kg (or 1.0lb if the meet is run in pounds)"

## Coaches

**Matt Gary, interviewed by Eric Helms and Mike Zourdos, MASS Research Review, June 2023.** https://www.strongerbyscience.com/interview-matt-gary/

- "So what the data reveal is that an opener, typically it's gonna be somewhere between 90 to 92% of 1RM. I find that is our sweet spot, or the number that I kind of like to use is right in the middle, about 91% for most people. You know that's gonna be somewhere around your best triple. So it's whatever you can do for three reps to competition standard. So that's a good place, particularly for a novice to start if they don't know or have never tested a true 1RM, is take whatever you can get as your best triple to competition standard. That usually translates to a safe place to open, regardless of discipline."
- "The second attempt is typically gonna be somewhere between 95% to 97% 1RM." "However, I don't recommend going any heavier than 97% of 1RM on a second attempt, because ..."
- "So we don't really assign percentages to third attempts. Like I said, it's situationally based. So if the second attempt moves well and it feels good and the lifter is confident, then heck yeah, let's load it up and go for that incremental PB, that next 2.5 kg increment above their current PR. Alternatively, if the second attempt was tough, if it was hard or if it was sluggish, if it didn't move well, ... then you're basically picking based on the second attempt to potentially tie your PB or PR."
- "If your lifter misses the opener on strength, we're obviously gonna repeat that attempt, right?" On a miss for "something silly like a rack command": "then we might go ahead and move up to the second attempt".
- "So making nine out of nine is better than eight out of nine and is better than seven."

**Matt Gary, "A Powerlifter's Guide to Attempt Selection", 11 May 2009.** https://mass-lift.com/blog/2009/05/11/a-powerlifters-guide-to-attempt-selection/

- "Your opener should usually be between 90-92% of your one-rep max (competition PR)."
- "the opener should be easy enough that you can make the lift on your worst day or if something goes wrong."
- "Have light, medium, and heavy options for your second and third attempts. If your warm-ups don't go as planned, lower your opener."
- "In the event that you miss your opener – repeat it." "It's one thing if you beat a “rack” command or take an extra step because the weight was so easy. Even then, I'm an advocate of repeating the opening attempt."
- "the second attempt is typically somewhere between 95-97% and no higher than 98% of your max."
- "Always make weight increases incrementally smaller."
- "If you are going for a PR, choose the next five-pound (2.5kg) increment."
- "If your second attempt felt heavy or was more difficult than you anticipated, take what's there – not what you want."

**Chad Wesley Smith, "3 Tips for Your 1st Meet", Juggernaut Training Systems, 22 October 2015.** https://www.jtsstrength.com/5-tips-for-your-1st-meet/

- "Pick conservative openers-These will be your most nerve wracking lifts so make them a number that you can hit under any circumstance, about 90-92% of your best CLEAN (deep, paused, full lockout, etc) lift in the gym during that training cycle is a good choice."
- "Good attempt selection guidelines for a first meet would be about: Opener: 90-92% / 2nd: 95-97% / 3rd: 99-102%"

**Eduardo Chile, "First Meet: Getting Ready for your Opener", Reactive Training Systems, 2 November 2016** (archived copy). https://web.archive.org/web/20170709094214/http://articles.reactivetrainingsystems.com/2016/11/02/first-meet-getting-ready-for-your-opener/

- "Your opener should be relatively easy and account for meet nerves. If you fail a weight for any reason, you should likely repeat it and then go up from there."
- Chart, labelled "Percent": squat 87–89%, 94–96%, 99–102%; bench 88–90%, 94–96%, 99–102%; deadlift 87–89%, 94–96%, 99–102%.

## Studies

**Travis SK, Zourdos MC, Bazyler CD. Weight Selection Attempts of Elite Classic Powerlifters. Perceptual and Motor Skills 2021;128(1):507–521.** https://doi.org/10.1177/0031512520967608

- Male (66) and female (43) lifters "who completed all lifts successfully at an International Powerlifting Federation Classic World Championship between 2012-2019".
- "selecting an opener of ∼91% of the expected A3 weight, followed by ∼5% increase from A1 to A2, and ∼3% increase from A2 to A3 represent the typical weight selection attempts used across lifts by elite classic powerlifters competing in the World Championships."

**Howells RJ, Spathis JG, Pearson J, Latella C, Garrett JM, Owen PJ, van den Hoek DJ. Impacts of squat attempt weight selection and success on powerlifting performance. Journal of Sports Medicine and Physical Fitness 2022;62(4):476–484.** https://doi.org/10.23736/S0022-4707.21.12140-1

- 10,672 entries from "'raw' Powerlifting Australia sanctioned competitions held between 2008 and 2019". On the squat: "athletes selected opening attempts which were an average of 92% of their achieved maximum on the day with 93.5% of competitors improving on this weight in subsequent attempts."

**van den Hoek D, and colleagues. What are the odds? Identifying factors related to competitive success in powerlifting. BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation 2022;14:110.** https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-022-00505-2

- Raw/classic Australian meets, 2010 to 2019, 10,599 entries.
- "less than 50% of athletes achieve a successful lift at their third attempt for the BP."
- "coaches may consider a moderated strategy which targets opening attempts above the 91% of one repetition maximum typically used, in favour of attempts weights typically chosen at attempt two instead (i.e. ~ 95–97% of one repetition maximum). We acknowledge that this suggestion differs from current coaching strategies, thus we recommend that the adoption of such an approach (based on the current results) should be trialled during training and/or recreational competitions prior to possible implementation at higher level tournaments."

**van den Hoek D, Garrett J, Travis SK, Oxnard K, Howells R, Owen P, Latella C. Analysis of Competition Performance Leading to Success at the International Powerlifting Federation World Championships Between 2013 and 2019. Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research 2023;37(10):e555–e562.** https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000004488

- 1,312 lifters at open IPF World Championships: "The odds of winning a competition were increased 5.9 times for men and by 1.5 times for women, when successfully completing 8 or 9 of 9 lift attempts."

**Darragh IAJ, Egan B, Nolan D, Bennett KE. Predicting a Successful Attempt in Raw Powerlifting. Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research 2026;40(2):e138–e146.** https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000005276

- 93,333 lifters across 6,979 IPF competitions: "In both sexes, third attempts consistently had lower success rates than second attempts."
